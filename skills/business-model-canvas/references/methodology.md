# Model diagnostics

Original method: nine interdependent blocks (Osterwalder/Pigneur). The following evidence tags and test register are project operating conventions, not extra original Canvas blocks.

| Check | Diagnostic question | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Segment/value | Which named actor receives which benefit? | Separate incompatible segments instead of averaging them. |
| Channel | How is that actor reached, sold to, and served? | An audience-size assertion is not a demonstrated acquisition path. |
| Relationship | Who handles acquisition, support, and retention? | Self-service and intensive manual support imply different activities/costs. |
| Revenue | Who pays what, for which unit, at what frequency? | Price alone does not imply volume or contribution. |
| Delivery | Which resources, activities, and partners enable that promise? | An uncontracted critical partner is a dependency, not a verified asset. |
| Economics | Are revenue and cost measured per matching unit and period? | Unknown CAC, retention, or service costs prevent profit claims. |
| Platform | Who subsidizes whom, and why will each side join? | Map both sides and the exchange; do not force one fictional customer. |

A multisided platform may be one connected canvas with explicitly side-labeled entries. Separate canvases are useful for genuinely different models; never separate interdependent platform sides so far that the exchange disappears. A later analyst's public case does not establish founder use of the tool.

When inputs are sparse, mark unknown blocks and propose the next discriminating experiment. A qualitative consequence/evidence rationale is sufficient; do not invent a numeric risk matrix.
