# Calibration examples — synthetic teaching fixtures

These are invented business situations, not documented customer research. Do not reuse their numbers or conclusions as user evidence.

## Correct: one service model
Input F1 (user report): a bookkeeping service sells to small clinics for 200 units/month. F2: the founder performs onboarding manually. F3: clinics are recruited through a local association; no acquisition counts or labor costs are supplied.
Procedure: label all three reports as unverified; distinguish clinic decision maker, beneficiary staff, and payer. Draft all nine blocks; identify manual onboarding as an activity requiring labor, not a free channel feature. Mark partner access and conversion unproven.
Output excerpt: revenue = reported subscription mechanism, not proven recurring revenue; resources = founder time (capacity unknown); costs = labor/acquisition unknown. Contradiction: scalable self-service claim versus manual setup. Next test: observe delivery time and test one recruitment cohort under a proposed, explicitly non-factual decision threshold. No margin claim is possible.

## Incorrect: filled cells treated as validation
Input: founder says everyone needs an app, price is 20, expected users 10,000.
Bad result: monthly revenue is confirmed at 200,000 and product-market fit is established.
Correction: demand and volume are hypotheses. The product and price do not supply a customer segment, reachable channel, or costs. A scenario calculation may be labeled as a scenario, never a forecast. Return unknown blocks and tests for segment/value/payer before growth claims.

## Edge: two-sided platform
Input: learners use a marketplace free; tutors pay a commission only on completed lessons. Payment/refund costs and tutor recruitment are unknown.
Procedure: label learner and tutor sides in every relevant block; trace trust, matching, payment, and refunds to activities/costs. Identify learner value and tutor payer role separately. An increase in free users alone does not validate tutor-side economics.
Useful result: one connected platform map plus a dependency audit and tests for paid completed lessons, repeat use, and acquisition/service costs. Keep quantities unknown until observed.
