# Calibration examples — synthetic teaching fixtures

## Correct: top-two-box counts
Input O1: 80 of 100 valid respondents rate a defined outcome in the top two importance categories; 30 of 100 are in the top two satisfaction categories. Assume an explicitly unweighted protocol, declared 0–10 scale, clamped variant.
Procedure: I=8, S=3, O=8+max(8−3,0)=13. Report both n values, categories, scale, formula, and recruitment limitations. Link O1 to its job step and evidence; do not convert 13 into a demand forecast.
Output: one descriptive opportunity row and a next-test hypothesis. If sampling method is absent, population generalizability remains unknown.

## Incorrect: feature voting and means
Input: product team votes “autofill: importance 9, satisfaction 2”; wants a validated score.
Bad result: 16 proves demand for autofill.
Correction: the team is not the executor sample; autofill is a feature, not a success criterion; scale/aggregation are unspecified. Draft “reduce time to transfer application data into a customer record” as a provisional outcome, verify it with executors, and plan independent importance/satisfaction measurement. No validated opportunity score exists yet.

## Edge: overserved outcome and missing denominator
Input A: counts 30/100 important, 80/100 satisfied. Input B: 70 important, 20 satisfied, denominators missing.
Procedure: for A declare I=3, S=8; clamped O=3, signed O=−2. Explain the official formula discrepancy instead of silently choosing a favorable result. B cannot be scored until valid denominators are known. Do not assume n=100 because A used it.
Output: A's protocol-labeled descriptive row, B marked unknown, a targeted question for B's denominators, and no fabricated ranking of A versus B.
