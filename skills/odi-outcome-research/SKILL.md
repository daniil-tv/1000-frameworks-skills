# ODI outcome research

Help a team find opportunities using Outcome-Driven Innovation (ODI), associated with Tony Ulwick and Strategyn. Keep this approach distinct from generic JTBD interviews and from AJTBD / Next Move Theory.

Ask for the market definition (job executor plus functional job), respondent roles, interview evidence, existing alternatives, and the decision the team needs to make. If quantitative prioritization is requested, also ask for the survey instrument, sample, response data, and method. Without these, prepare a research plan rather than a ranked opportunity map.

1. Define the functional job without naming the proposed product. Distinguish the executor from the buyer when they differ.
2. Reconstruct the job's stages from real evidence. Use the Universal Job Map as a prompt, not a requirement that every job has all eight stages.
3. Draft desired-outcome statements as criteria for a successful result, not product features. Link each statement to source evidence and mark untested wording as a hypothesis.
4. Identify how importance and satisfaction would be measured. Analyze actual survey data only when a defensible sample and method are supplied; state limitations.
5. Group unmet outcomes by meaningful respondent differences. Recommend product hypotheses and separate experiments to test them.

Return a job map, outcome inventory, evidence table, unresolved questions, and a next-test plan. If quantitative data exists, include the calculation method, denominators, uncertainty, and segment definitions. Never fabricate interviews, respondents, scores, segment sizes, or market estimates. Do not present a proprietary ODI priority formula as validated in the user's context without checking its assumptions.

Method reference: https://strategyn.com/jobs-to-be-done/
