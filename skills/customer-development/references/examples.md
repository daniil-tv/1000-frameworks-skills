# Calibration examples — synthetic teaching fixtures

## Correct: predeclared decision
Input E1: team hypothesized that at least 3 of 10 eligible clinic managers would pay for a limited pilot in two weeks. E2: exactly 2 of the 10 paid. E3: 6 praised the demo. Costs/renewals unknown.
Procedure: compare actual payments with the original rule; keep praise separate from payments; inspect eligibility and recruitment.
Output: the proposed 3/10 pilot gate was not met (2/10). Positive comments do not rescue it. This does not prove nobody wants the service. Investigate objections and propose a revised offer or segment test, preserving the original result. Repeatability and profitability remain untested.

## Incorrect: agreeable interviews become demand
Input: 8 friendly contacts said they would use an app; no task observation, price commitment, or external recruitment.
Bad result: 80% of the market will buy; proceed to scalable acquisition.
Correction: show user-reported hypothetical interest from a biased sample. Form falsifiable role/problem/payer claims and test actual behavior or commitments before Validation. Do not fabricate a quota of interviews that guarantees success.

## Edge: one paying customer
Input: one founder acquaintance buys a bespoke pilot, demanding extensive manual work. The team asks to scale marketing.
Procedure: identify the confounding relationship and manual delivery; separate a sale from a repeatable sales process; record missing acquisition and labor economics.
Output: one conditional commitment supports further testing, not a scalable model. Test another eligible cohort under defined acquisition conditions and measure repeat use and service effort. A decision threshold must be proposed before this next test, not retroactively attached to the first sale.
