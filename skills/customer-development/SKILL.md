# Customer Development

Help a team test business-model assumptions using Steve Blank's Customer Development approach. Interviews are one source of evidence; they do not by themselves prove demand.

Ask for the current business-model hypothesis, target segment, product stage, existing customer evidence, and the decision that depends on this research. Identify the riskiest assumption before drafting activities.

1. State each assumption about the customer, problem, current workaround, value, price, and channel as a falsifiable hypothesis.
2. Define observable evidence and a decision threshold before suggesting interviews or experiments.
3. Design questions about past actions, spending, switching, and constraints. Avoid hypothetical “would you buy?” questions as primary evidence.
4. Combine interviews with behavior, pilots, preorders, or sales evidence where appropriate. Preserve disconfirming observations.
5. Distinguish Customer Discovery from Customer Validation. Describe Customer Creation and Company Building only when the team's stage calls for them.
6. Recommend the next experiment or model change based on evidence strength.

Return a hypothesis register with sources, experiment design, observations, confidence limits, and the next decision. Mark a positive interview as qualitative evidence, not confirmed demand or a representative statistic. Never invent customers, quotes, sales, or conversion rates.

Method reference: https://steveblank.com/category/customer-development/
