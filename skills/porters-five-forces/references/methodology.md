# Methodology and diagnostic boundaries

The original five forces are rivalry among existing competitors, threat of new entrants, threat of substitute products/services, bargaining power of buyers and bargaining power of suppliers. The framework evaluates the competitive structure and potential profitability of an industry. A firm's position and activity choices can produce different outcomes within that structure; a high-growth industry's revenues need not imply strong retained profits.

Start at a product/geography/customer/value-chain level where the competitive mechanisms are comparable. Test boundaries by asking who the buyer is, which suppliers/input exchanges matter, which products compete directly and which other means meet the same need. A participant's role is relative to the focal exchange. For a marketplace, distinguish merchant, end buyer, payment provider and platform; do not collapse every role into 'competitor'. Multiple force effects from one driver require explicit channels, not double-counting identical cost impacts.

A rival offers within the defined industry; a substitute meets the underlying job differently. Fulfillment in-house can substitute for outsourced fulfillment; another outsourced fulfillment operator is a rival. A substitute matters only where its economics, feasibility and switching burden make it a credible alternative.

Low entry today does not establish a barrier: threatening entrants may already cap price/investment choices. Test actual scale, access and capabilities rather than equating popularity with easy entry. Incumbent advantages can vary by niche. Rivalry depends on conduct and economics (growth, differentiation, capacity, costs, exit) as well as concentration; a few similar competitors can fight intensely.

Buyer size does not automatically override high switching costs. Supplier uniqueness does not by itself establish bargaining leverage if the resource is not material, alternatives exist or contracts constrain terms. Keep countervailing evidence and compare exposures; absent data is unknown, not a low-force rating.

The seven-step article workflow and evidence-status register are editorial project conventions for applying the model, not an official numbered Porter certification sequence. Qualitative or ordinal judgments support discussion but are not measurements prescribed by a canonical numerical formula. Do not infer real profitability from an unvalidated weighted force score.

Industry analysis informs choices, not guaranteed prescriptions. Distinct customer value and compatible activities involve costs and trade-offs. Before proposing differentiation/integration, identify a target segment, what will change, how it affects a force and why the company might execute it. Demand research, cost/revenue models and capability testing remain needed for investment or operating decisions.

Classical Five Forces has five forces. Government, complementors, technology and macroeconomic shifts can alter barriers, rivalry or leverage; discussing extensions is permitted if clearly attributed and distinguished from the original model. Broad PESTEL trends alone do not constitute industry analysis. The value chain disaggregates firm activities and sources of advantage, a separate unit of analysis.
