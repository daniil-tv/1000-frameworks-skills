# Synthetic calibration examples

## Evidence-led bounded build
Synthetic inputs: Outcome O1: improve completed/started applications among first-time applicants over a defined fortnight; current baseline unknown. Interview I1, episode e2: an applicant searched for a required document, abandoned the form and phoned support. Interview I2: another applicant was unsure whether a previously uploaded document was accepted. These are participant accounts, not market estimates.
A plausible parent need is 'Know what remains to finish my application', with children 'Know which documents are required' [I1:e2] and 'Know whether an uploaded document was accepted' [I2]. The connection to O1 remains a causal hypothesis. A learning priority can be the first child because its abandonment episode is more direct; this does not establish that it is more prevalent. Proposed alternatives: document checklist before starting; contextual example at the relevant step; an assisted document-readiness review. Test whether target users understand and prepare the right documents, with an agreed observable criterion and explicit next actions. Do not declare any option a retention winner from these inputs.

## Feature backlog correction
'Push notifications' is a solution. Do not rewrite it as an established opportunity called 'Customers need notifications'. Ask what recent event or difficulty led to the request. If a supplied support episode says 'I repeatedly refresh because I cannot tell if the order was accepted', retain that account, propose a need around status confidence, and compare notifications with an on-page state explanation or workflow change. Without the episode, mark the need as a hypothesis and propose story interviews.

## Conflicting evidence and inconclusive tests
I3 wants fast submission, I4 needs enough time to review legal terms. Preserve the tension and distinguish situations; do not average them into 'everyone wants fewer steps'. A prototype test where two participants like an auto-submit button does not prove safe consent or conversion impact. Review comprehension/safety and actual task behavior, including disconfirming cases. If logging is incomplete, classify a test inconclusive and specify missing exposure/outcome data rather than calling it successful.

## Quotation fidelity and a concrete screening decision
If source I1 literally says 'I wish I could confidently share a result with my colleague', keep those exact words when quoting I1. A candidate need such as Share results confidently can be an attributed paraphrase without quotation marks; never present the changed wording as a verbatim quote.
For an illustrative, not-yet-run checklist comprehension test: proposed screening criterion — each participant identifies every mandatory document for an assigned realistic scenario without researcher help. Record observed mistakes; a critical missing mandatory document means revise, not pass. If a prototype or answer key is broken, mark inconclusive and repair it. This qualitative criterion is a proposed project choice, not an OST universal rule or evidence of conversion lift. Define participants/exposure and verify the answer key before running.
