# Calibration examples — synthetic teaching fixtures

## Correct: traceable episode
Input transcript T1, L1: “In March I compared A and B.” L2: “I ordered B in May because A's exports kept failing.” L3: “I feared moving our archive; we kept A for a month.”
Procedure: March comparison and May order are reported events; first thought and precise day are unknown. Failing exports plausibly support Push; archive concern supports Anxiety. Retaining A indicates transition risk/partial use; it does not prove Habit strength.
Output excerpt: timeline rows cite T1:L1–L3; alternatives are A and B, not unmentioned competitors. Four-force table leaves Pull/Habit unconfirmed where evidence is insufficient. Next check asks what specifically attracted the buyer to B and when active search began. No quote is invented.

## Incorrect: made-up completeness
Input: “I bought B last week; I like the interface.”
Bad result: first thought occurred in January, three competitors were evaluated, Habit was 2/10, conversion will rise 30%.
Correction: retain only the reported purchase and present preference. Preference is not established historical cause. Ask neutral follow-ups; missing stages/forces remain unknown. Do not turn a neat template into fabricated research.

## Edge: inconsistent B2B accounts

Procedure: build actor-tagged April/June/July rows, preserve the chronology conflict, distinguish first awareness from formal procurement. Do not silently replace one account.
Output: unresolved explanation plus a neutral follow-up and request for existing procurement notes. Cross-episode claims and market frequencies are unavailable from this one case.
