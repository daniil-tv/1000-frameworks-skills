# Primary sources — checked 2026-11-24

- Alexander Osterwalder, Yves Pigneur, Greg Bernarda, Alan Smith, *Value Proposition Design*, Wiley, 2014. Bibliographic foundation; no claim of reading the entire book for this release.
- Strategyzer, [The Customer Profile](https://www.strategyzer.com/library/the-customer-profile): Jobs, Pains and Gains; one half of the canvas.
- Strategyzer, [The Value Map](https://www.strategyzer.com/library/the-value-map): Products & Services, Pain Relievers and Gain Creators; mechanisms of value creation.
- Benson Garner, Strategyzer, 19 February 2015, [5 Common Mistakes to Avoid When Using the Value Proposition Canvas](https://www.strategyzer.com/library/5-common-mistakes-to-avoid-when-using-the-value-proposition-canvas): separating the halves and segments, avoiding product-biased customer profiles.
- Nabila Amarsy, Strategyzer, 17 February 2015, [Roadmap to Test Your Value Proposition](https://www.strategyzer.com/library/roadmap-to-test-your-value-proposition): testing profile, offering and willingness to pay. Ads and clicks mentioned in the article are evidence of interest, not sufficient payment evidence or profitability; that evidential distinction is this project's analytical interpretation.
- Frederic Etiemble, Strategyzer, 25 March 2019, [Are Gains the Opposite of Pains?](https://www.strategyzer.com/library/are-gains-the-opposite-of-pains): expectations, continua and boundaries; gains are not simply inverted pains.

The online sources were read for the scope and diagnostics above. Their templates remain subject to the rights holder's terms. Evidence statuses, IDs, contradiction tables and test thresholds in this skill are explicit project analysis conventions. They are not quoted as official VPC metrics.
