# Methodology and diagnostics

## Boundary

VPC, developed by Alexander Osterwalder, Yves Pigneur and Alan Smith within Strategyzer, zooms into the customer segment–value proposition link of the Business Model Canvas. It has two halves and six elements, not the BMC's nine blocks. A text/table reconstruction of analytical findings is sufficient; no proprietary visual template is required.

## Customer versus offer

Customer Jobs describe work in context, including functional, social and emotional aspects. “Use our dashboard” is a solution, not a job. Ask what the customer was trying to achieve and how they handled it before.

Customer Pains are difficulties, risks or undesirable outcomes in doing that work. Customer Gains are outcomes the customer values. Distinguish basic expectations from differentiating benefits. “No errors” does not automatically mean delight. A continuum can contain pain, acceptable baseline and useful gain, and an extreme can become harmful. Check the customer's actual expectation rather than assuming a mirror image.

Products & Services list the offer. Pain Relievers and Gain Creators explain its causal mechanisms against specific customer needs. A promised mechanism stays hypothetical until supported. One feature can address several needs only when each connection is explained; important needs can remain deliberately unaddressed.

## Audit checks

- Different users, payers or decision-makers may need different profiles. Record the exchange and dependence between them; do not average their needs into a fictional customer.
- Keep the profile independent of the chosen offer. Preserve needs that the product cannot address and evidence against the team's favorite hypothesis.
- Priorities need evidence of importance in context. Interview frequency is sample evidence, not market prevalence. Unknown importance is unknown, not a score to manufacture.
- Distinguish a logical design match from evidence of usefulness and a commercial commitment. A successful pilot may demonstrate one mechanism in one context without proving repeat demand or profit.
- Ask whether onboarding, switching effort, governance or a workflow change erases the promised benefit. A proposed feature can create a new pain.
- Differences between accounts may reflect role, context, timing or measurement. Keep alternatives open; do not erase a quote to reconcile a neat story.

## Test logic

First test whether the job/pain/gain matters; then whether the mechanism creates the promised outcome; then commercial commitment where relevant. This is an iterative research route, not a compulsory linear certification sequence. Choose the next test based on the unresolved decision and risk.

A useful test record is hypothesis → evidence needed → participants/context → observation or measure → predeclared proposed criterion → actual result (unknown until executed) → decision. Separate failure of recruitment, insufficient data and a failed hypothesis. Do not convert an inconclusive test into validation.

Evidence-status labels, ID-based traceability, fit tables and proposed thresholds are project conventions for disciplined analysis, not official numerical VPC metrics. They do not authorize fabricated data or imply causal certainty.
