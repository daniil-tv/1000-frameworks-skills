# Calibration — synthetic examples, not validated case studies

## Useful provisional build
A fictional warehouse-software team supplies U1: one owner-operated seller wants faster picking; U2: one fulfillment manager needs multi-client billing; U3: current software has stock tracking but no multi-client billing. These are attributed individual reports, not market segment counts. Candidate groups might differ by workflow/job, with observable inclusion rules. Compare all five criteria and mark unknown reach, demand and value. Fulfillment may look attractive but lack product fit; the team can either investigate a feasible owner-operated niche or test the cost of adding billing. No profitable winner is proven. A positioning draft may promise only existing tracking, with the alleged time benefit pending pilot measurement. Validation checks customer comprehension and alternatives, use/payment, service economics and delivery capacity.

## Faulty shortcut and repair
'Women35–45 are a segment, choose them because largest, promise50% faster setup from our team's map.' This conflates descriptors, priorities and proof. Investigate different purchase situations/criteria and meaningful actions; compare access and capabilities with market potential; leave size and speed as unknown without sources; test positioning with actual customers and behavior.

## Conflicting evidence
Sales reports effortless migration; two users describe lengthy setup. Keep both sources and role/context differences. A target may be reachable but expensive to implement. Prefer a bounded learning decision, measure actual setup/service costs, and revise positioning to remove unsupported simplicity claims. Different roles need separate profiles when their decision needs differ, not automatically one independent market segment per role.
