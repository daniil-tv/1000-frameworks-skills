# Worked examples (synthetic learning inputs)

## Comparable raw observations

Scenario: a local buyer compares two pickup plans, same location and date. S1 tariff shows A at 20 currency units and B at 40 per month; S2 observation shows A pickup in 2 days and B same day. Both plans' support hours are unknown. The buyer wants to compare price level, pickup speed and support availability.

Return the raw facts with S1/S2, clarify tax/usage comparability if needed, and propose factor-specific ordinal anchors if a relative curve is required. A high price level means more expensive; faster pickup means a higher speed offering. Do not assign support 3/5. A plot-ready matrix can retain unknown and a proposed mapping pending agreement. A future same-day low-price plan is hypothetical; require cost/capacity and choice tests before recommending it.

## Flawed profile audit

Input claims a competitor is 5/5 for quality because its website says best in class, takes price 5/5 to mean cheap in one line and expensive in another, and fills unknowns with 3. A bold zigzag future curve supposedly guarantees demand.

Identify unverified marketing claim, inconsistent direction, imputation and invalid causal inference. Preserve the website claim as a claim, mark unsupported levels unknown, agree scale/anchors, and separate a hypothesized curve. Do not replace bad numbers with other invented numbers.

## Boundary and narrow result

A user asks whether an enterprise annual contract belongs on a graph with personal monthly subscriptions. Explain the segment/choice and bundle mismatch; separate comparisons unless there is evidence the same buyer genuinely chooses between them and a defensible common usage basis. If the only request is to translate “value curve,” return the translation rather than invoking a complete strategy workflow.
