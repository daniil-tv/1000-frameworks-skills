# Calibration examples — synthetic, not market research

## Correct scoped application
A fictional warehouse operator considers one new site in region R over two years. Input E1 is a supplied 5% financing assumption, not a central-bank observation; E2 is a planning note about possible route disruption, not a forecast. Economic exposure: a 2 percentage-point rate-rise scenario on an assumed 10m interest-only balance adds 200,000 annual interest, conditional on the balance and timing. It does not predict the rate or prove project profitability. Environmental exposure: route interruption could reduce service capacity; validate route-specific data. Proposed action: model financing and route alternatives. Monitor: responsible finance/operations roles review specified sources when relevant rates or route restrictions change; thresholds require calibration. Unresearched categories stay evidence gaps.

## Incorrect application and repair
'Weak sales team' is internal. 'Inflation increases, therefore enter this country, attractiveness 87/100' lacks date, causal exposure and justified scoring. Repair: investigate dated inflation evidence and how price/cost exposure changes margin; keep capability diagnosis outside PESTEL, and require demand, competition and financial sensitivity before investment.

## Incomplete or contradictory evidence
One source predicts 4% growth; another predicts 1%, with different publication dates or populations. Keep both as attributed forecasts, check comparability, and use a range/scenarios where justified. A policy proposal is monitored politically, not treated as an effective legal obligation. If the question concerns one customer's canceled order, a customer interview rather than macro scanning may be appropriate.
