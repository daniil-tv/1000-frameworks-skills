# Methodology and decision rules

PESTEL scans external macro conditions; Porter's Five Forces examines industry competitive structure; SWOT combines internal strengths/weaknesses with external opportunities/threats; Business Model Canvas describes how the business creates, delivers and captures value. These can complement one another but cannot be substituted without changing the question.

Political: government policy, trade direction, stability, tariffs and subsidy priorities. Legal: specific obligations, scope, compliance deadlines and enforcement. An enacted tariff can be relevant to both; keep one exposure record and cross-reference it. Verify the legally operative instrument before treating a proposal as an obligation.
Economic: rates, inflation, exchange, income, employment and cost of capital; show effects on both demand and costs, comparability and lags.
Social: demographic/behavioral changes supported by research; national averages do not establish a narrow segment's behavior.
Technological: availability, adoption, economics, integration and skills; a demonstration is not production adoption.
Environmental: physical climate/resource exposures and sustainability expectations; distinguish physical effects, voluntary demands and enacted duties.

A fact is a sourced observation or adopted measure. A forecast is a dated expectation attributed to a source and method. A scenario is a possible state used to explore consequences, with no probability implied. A decision is the company's response. A press report saying 'may adopt' is not an adopted rule. Preserve contradictions and vintage differences instead of silently selecting a convenient number.

No canonical mandatory final numerical score or universal update interval exists. Separate significance, confidence and timing; numerical judgments need a named scale, rationale and uncertainty. Scenario financial calculations require supplied assumptions and remain conditional. Prioritize even small immediate obligations over large remote possibilities when the decision requires it. A single driver's cross-category effects must not be added as independent shocks without justification.

The seven-step workflow on the encyclopedia page (decision, signals, changes, causal chain, significance, options, monitoring) is a practical editorial procedure consistent with environmental scanning, not a uniquely prescribed original algorithm. Modern six-category PESTEL has no universally recognized sole inventor. Aguilar's 1967 book used ETPS (Economic, Technical, Political, Social), an important predecessor; do not attribute today's six-category arrangement directly to him.

A useful monitor specifies what changes, where the evidence comes from, who reviews it and which decision is reconsidered. Cadence depends on volatility and decision reversibility. Exact thresholds are to be calibrated using the user's decision economics, not invented to make a table appear complete.
